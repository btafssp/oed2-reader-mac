Symbols and Abbreviations

Official wording from the Oxford English Dictionary. Section 1 quotes Symbols and other conventions on OED Online, with supplementary sentences from James Murray’s General Explanations. Section 2 is the Key to Second Edition pronunciation transcriptions. Section 3 combines the OED Online Abbreviations list with the printed List of Abbreviations, Signs, &c. from the First Edition front matter.

1. Entry signs and date marks

From OED Online, Symbols and other conventions:

Before a word or meaning

†= obsolete

In an etymology

*indicates a word or form not actually found, but of which the existence is inferred
<= from
>= developed into or borrowed as

Within a forms section

α., β., and other letters of the Greek alphabetindicate lists of forms that are in some way similar or related. For example, in the case of fantastic adj. and n. the α. and β are used to separate the forms that begin with the letter f- and those that begin with ph-. The letters will also appear in the quotations sections alongside the illustrative examples of the similar or related forms.

Before a date

a= ante (‘before’)
c= circa (‘approximately’)
?indicates an uncertain date

Within a definition

=means ‘the same as’

Within the quotations section

‥indicates an omitted part of a quotation
[ ]surrounds an editorial insertion
~indicates a hyphen introduced in the printing of the First Edition of the OED, which may not have been present in the cited text

Around an entire quotation

[ ]indicates that a quotation is relevant to the development of a meaning but not directly illustrative of it

Symbols used in previous editions of the OED

“The following are symbols used in previous editions of the OED: print editions and/or the Second Edition entries which can be viewed online.”

‖This symbol means that the word was not considered to be naturalized in English
¶This symbol means that the word or usage was considered catachrestic or erroneous
[ ]Square brackets around an entire entry indicate a ‘spurious’ entry, for example a word which, on investigation, turned out to be a misreading
–0This symbol after the label Obs. (Obsolete) or rare indicates a word or meaning for which no contextual examples from printed or written sources were available to the editors
–1This symbol after the label Obs. (Obsolete) or rare indicates a word or meaning for which only one contextual example from a printed or written source was available to the editors
–2This symbol after the label Obs. (Obsolete) or rare indicates a word or meaning for which only two contextual examples from printed or written sources were available to the editors

“The following abbreviations were formerly used in lists of variant spellings:”

1= before 1100
2= 12th century (1100–1200)
3= 13th century (1200–1300)
4= 14th century (1300–1400)
5-8= 15th to 18th century
9-= 19th century to present
20= 20th century

From Murray’s General Explanations

† and ‖Words believed to be obsolete are distinguished by prefixing †; non-naturalized or partially-naturalized words, by ‖.
† before a senseObsolete Senses, like obsolete words, have † prefixed, so as to be at once distinguished from those now in use.
Forms numberingThe principal earlier Forms or Spellings, with their chronological range indicated by the unit figure of the century, thus 3–6 = 13th to 16th cent.; 1 standing for all centuries down to 1100.
rare —¹ / —⁰Here also is added, when applicable, the epithet rare, with —¹, or —⁰, indicating that only one, or no actual instance of the use of the word is known to us.
SMALL CAPITALSThe printing of a word in SMALL CAPITALS indicates that further information will be found under the word so referred to.

Symbols in parentheses are used to denote elements that may be omitted either by individual speakers or in particular phonetic contexts: e.g. bottle /ˈbɒt(ə)l/, Mercian /ˈmɜːʃ(ɪ)ən/, suit /s(j)uːt/, impromptu /ɪmˈprɒm(p)tjuː/, father /ˈfɑːðə(r)/.

The incidence of main stress is shown by a superior stress mark (ˈ) preceding the stressed syllable, and a secondary stress by an inferior stress mark (ˌ), e.g. accentuation /ækˌsɛntjuːˈeɪʃən/.
